# Patient Landing-Page Copy Review and Updated Variations

## Editorial Assessment

The supplied material has three useful directions: an evidence-heavy version, a conversational version, and a conversion-led version. The strongest patient experience combines the **warm recognition** of the conversational version with the **clear next step** of the conversion-led version.

The current draft is much longer than a patient landing page needs to be. Several ideas repeat across the tiredness, snoring, brain-fog, symptom-question, and closing sections. The most fear-led passages—especially the extended numerical risk list and sudden-cardiac-death discussion—should not lead the page. They may increase anxiety, distract from the survey, and imply an individual risk assessment that the page cannot provide.

The revised directions below keep the page medically responsible: symptoms can have many causes; a survey is not a diagnosis; and a custom oral appliance is only a possible discussion after appropriate evaluation.

> **Recommended page flow:** Symptom recognition → why the pattern is worth discussing → how evaluation works → possible oral-appliance conversation → survey / consultation handoff.

## Recommended Direction: Calm, Clear, and Conversion-Focused

### Hero

**Tired after a full night? Snoring? Finding it hard to focus?**

# Your sleep may be worth a closer look.

Waking tired, loud snoring, and daytime brain fog can have many causes. When they happen together, they may be connected to sleep-related breathing.

**Primary CTA:** Check my sleep symptoms

*A brief survey can help you decide whether a sleep evaluation is worth discussing. It does not diagnose a condition.*

### Symptom Recognition

## Do any of these sound familiar?

- Waking up tired, even after enough time in bed
- Loud or regular snoring
- Gasping, choking, or pauses in breathing noticed by a partner
- Morning headaches or dry mouth
- Daytime sleepiness, brain fog, or trouble focusing

If several feel familiar, the next step is not to guess. It is to understand what may be affecting your sleep.

**CTA:** See if my symptoms may be connected

### Why It Matters

## Sleep is not only about how long you are in bed.

Sleep can be less restorative when breathing is repeatedly interrupted. You may not remember those interruptions the next morning. You may simply notice the effects: low energy, a foggy head, or a partner who hears changes in your breathing.

This does not mean you have sleep apnea. It means your symptoms may deserve a closer look.

### Oral-Appliance Connection

## If a sleep-related breathing problem is confirmed, there may be options.

After appropriate evaluation and sleep testing, a qualified clinician can explain the next steps. For some adults, a custom oral appliance may be one treatment option. It is designed to support the airway during sleep, but it is not appropriate for everyone.

Your dentist and sleep-care team can help determine whether it is suitable for you.

### Closing

## Start with what you are experiencing.

You do not need to diagnose yourself. Tell us about your sleep, snoring, and daytime symptoms. We will help you understand whether a further evaluation may be appropriate.

**Primary CTA:** Request a consultation

*This page and survey are educational only. They do not diagnose sleep apnea or another medical condition.*

If you wake up tired, your partner hears loud snoring or breathing changes, and your focus fades during the day, a sleep-related breathing issue may be one possibility to discuss.

| Current content area | Recommended short replacement | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Fear-led risk-statistics block | **Why it is worth discussing:** Symptoms alone do not tell you the cause. They can be a reason to discuss an appropriate sleep evaluation. | **The goal is clarity, not alarm:** A survey cannot diagnose you, but it can help identify patterns worth discussing. |

## Recommended CTA Vocabulary

Use one primary phrase consistently across the page, with a secondary survey-specific label where the GoHighLevel handoff opens.

| Purpose | Preferred wording |
|---|---|
| Main conversion action | **Request a consultation** |
| Survey-specific action | **Check my sleep symptoms** |
| Evaluation framing | **See whether a sleep evaluation may be appropriate** |
| Avoid | “Find out if you are at risk,” “Do not ignore this,” “Nearly 4X,” “your cardiovascular system does not,” and other fear-forward language. |

## Patient-Safety Footer

> This page and survey are educational and do not diagnose sleep apnea or another medical condition. Snoring, fatigue, and brain fog can have many causes. A qualified healthcare professional and appropriate sleep testing are needed to diagnose sleep apnea and recommend treatment.
