# Oral-Appliance Content Boundaries — 2026-09-04

This note defines the patient-facing claims permitted in the Breathe Better Tonight landing-page update. It is a writing guardrail, not patient-specific medical advice.

## Approved concise points

| Topic | Patient-facing position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Symptoms worth discussing | Loud or frequent snoring, observed breathing pauses, gasping or choking sounds, waking unrefreshed, and daytime sleepiness or trouble focusing are useful details to bring to a clinician. | [AASM Sleep Education — Oral Appliance Therapy](https://sleepeducation.org/patients/oral-appliance-therapy/) · [Mayo Clinic — Obstructive sleep apnea: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/obstructive-sleep-apnea/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352095) |
| Why discuss a pattern | Symptoms can affect rest, daytime energy, or a partner's sleep, but can have more than one cause. The page must not diagnose a sleep disorder. | [Mayo Clinic — Obstructive sleep apnea: Diagnosis and treatment](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/obstructive-sleep-apnea/diagnosis-treatment/drc-20352095) |
| What an oral appliance is | A custom oral appliance is a removable device worn during sleep. Some devices move the lower jaw forward or support the tongue to help keep the airway open. | [AASM Sleep Education — Oral Appliance Therapy](https://sleepeducation.org/patients/oral-appliance-therapy/) |
| Appropriate pathway | For obstructive sleep apnea, a sleep physician diagnoses the condition through appropriate testing before a qualified dentist fits an oral appliance. A custom, titratable appliance and follow-up are the guideline-supported pathway. | [AASM/AADSM Oral Appliance Therapy Clinical Guideline](https://aasm.org/oral-appliance-therapy-clinical-guideline-published-jointly-by-aasm-and-aadsm/) · [AASM Sleep Education — Oral Appliance Therapy](https://sleepeducation.org/patients/oral-appliance-therapy/) |
| Candidate language | Oral appliances may be an option for some adults with diagnosed obstructive sleep apnea or primary snoring, depending on clinical assessment, preferences, and treatment needs. | [AASM/AADSM Oral Appliance Therapy Clinical Guideline](https://aasm.org/oral-appliance-therapy-clinical-guideline-published-jointly-by-aasm-and-aadsm/) |

## Claims intentionally excluded

The site must not state or imply that snoring diagnoses sleep apnea, that an appliance is right for every visitor, that it replaces an evaluation, that it cures sleep apnea, or that it guarantees better sleep, energy, focus, or partner comfort. It must not recommend over-the-counter mouth devices as treatment. The page must preserve an emergency-care instruction and direct visitors to an appropriate clinician for persistent concerns.
